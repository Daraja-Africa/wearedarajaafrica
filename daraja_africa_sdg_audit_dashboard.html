Executive Summary & Organizational Mandate

The intersection of youth development and mental health in Kenya represents one of the most pressing public health, economic, and human rights challenges of this decade. Operating as a youth-led organization in Nairobi, Daraja Africa Network bridges vulnerable university students and youth with peer support, psychoeducation, and community advocacy. While mental health touches virtually every dimension of sustainable development, a credible digital strategy requires focusing on a highly targeted set of SDGs: SDG 3 (Good Health and Well-being) and SDG 4 (Quality Education) as primary anchors, supported by SDG 5 (Gender Equality), SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities), and SDG 17 (Partnerships).

Advancing Global Goals Through Local Action

At Daraja Africa Network, we believe that the global mental health crisis cannot be solved without localized, culturally resonant, youth-led interventions. Our grassroots work across Kenya directly advances the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals by ensuring that psychological support is accessible, equitable, and community-driven.

SDG 3: Good Health and Well-being. We directly contribute to Target 3.4 by actively promoting mental health and well-being among Kenyan youth. Through our community-based peer support programs and accessible digital counseling frameworks, we work to reduce psychological distress, combat suicide stigma, and bridge the massive national gap in mental healthcare access.

SDG 4: Quality Education. True education extends beyond the academic classroom. We support Target 4.7 by equipping university students and marginalized youth with essential socio-emotional life skills, mental health literacy, and resilience training, empowering them to thrive in high-pressure environments.

SDG 10: Reduced Inequalities. Mental healthcare must not be a privilege reserved for a few in urban centers. By decentralizing mental health education and utilizing accessible digital platforms, we actively dismantle geographic and socio-economic barriers for vulnerable Kenyan youth.

SDG 17: Partnerships for the Goals. Systemic change requires radical collaboration. We partner with educational institutions, healthcare professionals, and civil society organizations to amplify our impact and build resilient, localized mental health networks.

UN Branding Rules & Evidence Impact Taxonomy

Essential compliance rules for non-UN entities using Sustainable Development Goal visuals, alongside a data-driven measurement framework to substantiate claims.

Prohibited

No UN Emblem (Logo Version 1)

External NGOs are strictly forbidden from using SDG Logo Version 1 containing the UN emblem. Daraja must exclusively use SDG Logo Version 2 (without emblem) and individual SDG icons.

Mandatory

Required Disclaimer Text

Whenever SDG icons or wheels are displayed, they MUST be accompanied by the explicit phrase: "[Daraja Africa Network] supports the Sustainable Development Goals".

Integrity

Visual Independence

SDG icons cannot be merged, morphed, or altered into the Daraja Africa Network logo. The organizational logo must always maintain primary visual prominence.
